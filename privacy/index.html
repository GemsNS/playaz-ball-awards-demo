Privacy

This page says what the Playaz Ball Awards website stores when you vote, buy a ticket or browse, and why.

Voting

To vote, the site stores a random voter ID in your browser. It is kept in a signed, httpOnly cookie named pba_voter, and it is how each browser gets one vote per category. The ID is a random value, not your name or email address, and scripts running on the page cannot read it. The cookie is set when you open the voting page and expires after 180 days.

Your IP address is never stored with your vote. With each vote the site stores two one-way keyed hashes (HMAC-SHA-256): one of your network address, which is your IP address or, on IPv6, the network part of it, and one of your browser’s user-agent string. They are used only to limit votes per network and to detect ballot stuffing. There is no way back from either hash to you, and nothing links a hash to which nominee you chose.

Like almost every website, the web server that delivers these pages keeps a standard access log, which does record IP addresses alongside the pages requested. That log is separate from the vote records above and is not used to work out how anyone voted.

No name, email address or account is required to vote.

Bot check

When the organizers turn it on, Cloudflare Turnstile checks that a vote comes from a person and not a bot. Turnstile is run by Cloudflare, in your browser, under Cloudflare’s privacy policy. To confirm the result, the site sends your IP address to Cloudflare along with each vote’s check.

Tickets

Tickets are sold by Eventbrite. You pay on Eventbrite’s checkout, and what you enter there is handled by Eventbrite under Eventbrite’s privacy policy.

When on-site checkout is turned on, your browser loads Eventbrite’s checkout script from eventbrite.com as the ticket section of the home page comes into view, or when you point at, tab to or touch a ticket button. Eventbrite then receives your IP address and browser details, even if you do not buy a ticket.

Analytics

The site includes Vercel Web Analytics, which sets no cookies and reports aggregated numbers. It only collects anything when the site is served from Vercel’s own hosting; on the address you are reading this from, it collects nothing. If the organizers enable it, the site also uses Google Analytics 4, which does set cookies.

Both are used to count page views, ticket-button clicks and voting activity. No personal data is sent in these events.

3D scene

The home page draws a 3D night scene on your device with WebGL. It is drawn by code that ships with this site, and nothing about how you move through it is sent anywhere. The scene does make one outside request: it asks Open-Meteo, a public weather service, what the weather is doing at the CN Tower, so the sky matches Toronto. That request carries no cookie and no page address, and the location it asks about is the tower’s, never yours — but like any web request it does reveal your IP address to that service. The scene is skipped, and a still picture takes its place, when your browser asks for data saving, when your device reports very little memory, and when WebGL is unavailable. If you have asked your device for reduced motion, the scene is still drawn but nothing in it moves.

What the site keeps on your device

Besides the voting cookie above, the site saves two small values in your browser’s session storage: the weather reading for the 3D scene, so it is not fetched again on every page, and a note that you have already seen the opening animation, so it does not play twice. Both are cleared when you close the tab, never leave your device, and hold nothing about you.

How long vote records are kept

We delete vote records, or anonymize them by removing the voter ID and both hashes, within 90 days after the event on Friday, October 9, 2026.
